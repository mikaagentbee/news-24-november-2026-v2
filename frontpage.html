World Court Convicts Myanmar of Genocide

And the world begins to act: Britain sanctions five generals and formally recognises the crime, as capitals react to The Hague’s landmark ruling.
The Peace Palace in The Hague, seat of the International Court of Justice, whose judgment on Myanmar reverberated through world capitals on Tuesday.
By our correspondent in The Hague · Additional reporting from London and Cox’s Bazar

THE HAGUE — The reckoning widened on Tuesday over the World Court’s judgment that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya, as Britain sanctioned five senior generals and formally recognised the crime.

The ruling, delivered last Thursday by nine votes to six in The Gambia’s case, made Myanmar the first state ever held directly responsible for genocide by the UN’s highest court. Its 2017 “clearance operations” in Rakhine State — killings, serious harm and measures to prevent births — were done with intent to destroy the Rohingya in part, the “only reasonable inference” from the evidence.

The court ordered prosecutions, reparations and the safe return of the hundreds of thousands who fled. Myanmar’s military rejected the judgment; survivors in Cox’s Bazar said justice would be measured in deeds, not words. Continued on Page A9

Climate · Antalya

Climate Summit Collapses Without a Pact

ANTALYA — The COP31 climate summit has ended without an agreement — the first collapse of UN climate talks since Copenhagen in 2009 — after two weeks of talks ran into overtime and split over two words: fossil fuels.

A draft “Antalya Pact” stripped of every mention of fossil fuels was rejected by the European Union, Panama, the Marshall Islands and Norway. Oil states led by Saudi Arabia and Russia refused any phase-out language at all.

“This has been the worst ever COP,” said Mohamed Adow of Power Shift Africa. UN chief António Guterres called the outcome “deeply disappointing” but insisted there was “no ambiguity in the science.” Page A12

Business · New York

Huang Restored at Nvidia; Nasdaq Leaps 6.5%

NEW YORK — Nvidia’s board has reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive, eleven days after his resignation — and Wall Street roared its approval.

The unanimous vote followed direct pressure from President Trump, who said he had “convinced” the board, and a share collapse that had wiped some $2.8 trillion from the chipmaker’s value since October. On Monday the stock leapt 30 per cent, restoring roughly $1 trillion, while the Nasdaq climbed 6.51 per cent.

Hours later, Washington blacklisted Singapore’s Megaspeed, suspected of diverting advanced chips to China. Page B1
